One ball, end to end

The game decides each ball once, at the moment you press, and then animates the result. The result can't change mid-animation, and a replay redraws exactly the same ball.

bowlerAI.js1. Over plan & fieldFrom the required rate, wickets left and the over number
bowlerAI.js2. Delivery specType, pace, length, line, swing and seam, aimed and then scattered
delivery.js3. Ball pathSolved in closed form at release: when it reaches the bat and how high
batting.js4. Your pressTiming error, plus how well the shot fits this ball
batting.js5. Miss → edge → cleanThree rolls in order: beaten? edged? Otherwise a clean hit with quality q
ballSim.js6. FlightSpeed, direction and launch angle, integrated at 240 Hz
fielding.js7. Field & runsWho reaches it first, then a catch roll, the throw time and the runs

With no press it's a leave, and only the laws check runs (bowled, LBW, pad, or safe). A miss in step 5 goes to the same check.

Ball types, lengths and lines

The bowler AI picks a weighted plan for each over, then a type, a length and a line for each ball. It aims at a random point inside the zone and then misses by up to ±0.35 m of length (±0.55 m for yorkers) and ±6 cm of line. About 3.5% of balls stray: a wide outside off, or one down the leg side.

Delivery types

Swing and seam are in metres. Positive moves away from the right-hander (toward off). Total movement = swing + seam, which feeds the miss and edge odds.

Lines (where it arrives at the stumps)

The length label comes from where the ball actually pitched, not where it was aimed. Height at the stumps rises from the full end of a band to the short end, plus ±12 cm of pitch variation (±17 cm for cutters).

  • A new batter (fewer than 2 balls faced) gets +2.5 outswinger and ×0.5 bouncer.

  • The field follows the plan, but the captain switches attack to balanced when the required rate is over 12, and death to balanced when it is under 6.

The read (NEXT BALL card and pitch ring) by difficulty

The card shows the bowler's aim, not the ball. The ring is offset by up to the zone error. A disguised ball is shown as a different type (a slower ball as an outswinger, an off-cutter as an inswinger, and so on).

Timing

Each shot has a swing time: the time from your key press until the bat meets the ball. Contact is when the ball crosses the bat's plane, which is closer to the bowler for front-foot shots and deeper for back-foot ones.

err = (press − offset) + swing − contactTime − bias seconds; < 0 early, > 0 late tq = exp(−(err / σ)²) timing quality, 1 = perfect, falls off as a Gaussian
Your offset is −48.6 ms cal. In the nets you tended to press about 49 ms early, so every press is now counted as if it came 48.6 ms later. The window is also ×1.093 wider than the code default.

The specials scale all three numbers by their window factor: sweep ×0.7, reverse sweep ×0.6, scoop ×0.55. The labels are only the HUD call. What actually matters is the continuous tq.

How long you have: release to contact

Pace is the real speed × the difficulty's pace factor (Easy 0.62, Medium 0.72, Hard 0.84). Back-foot contact is about 18 ms later than front-foot. Your press lands at contact − swing, so on Medium a good-length ball means pressing about 0.50 s after release for a 0.13 s drive.

What being early or late does

  • Direction drag: early pulls the ball to the leg side and late pushes it to the off side, by err × 5.5 rad/s × shot drift × (ball speed / 28). 40 ms early on a Medium cover drive is about −12°.
  • Uppish: more than 0.8σ early on a front-foot ground shot launches at 11–20° instead of along the ground.
  • Edges: late (err > 10 ms) tends to find the outside edge.
  • Power: q = tq × (0.3 + 0.7·fit) scales the speed off the bat (see Power).

Shot fit

Fit (0–1) measures how well this ball suits the shot you chose. It multiplies the shot's length suitability by its line band, using where the ball actually arrives at the bat, then applies height penalties.

fit = len[length] × bandFit(z at bat, line band) bandFit = 1 inside the band, then fades linearly to 0 over 0.24 m outside it × 0.35 front-foot shot with the ball above 1.15 m × 0.30 sweep / reverse / scoop with the ball above 0.9 m × 0.40 pull with the ball below 0.55 m

Length suitability (calibrated)

The line band is in metres at the bat. + is off side; 0 is middle stump; the stumps span ±0.18 m. The → key plays a flick if the ball pitched within 6 m of the stumps and a pull if it pitched further away.

Probabilities: miss, edge, clean

Before any dice roll, two hard checks: |err| > miss window means beaten. A ball arriving more than 1.7 m high means beaten by the bounce (unless you played a pull). After that, two rolls in sequence:

move = |swing + seam| metres missP = max(0, (1−fit)² × 0.736 × (1 − 0.4·tq) + move × 0.25 − 0.05) if fit < 0.10 → missP ≥ 0.85 the wrong shape entirely specials → missP += (1 − tq) × 0.35 edgeP = (0.02 + (1−fit) × 0.429 + move × 0.35) × (0.35 + 0.65·(1−tq)) + (1−tq) × 0.268 × 0.55 defence, + 0.05 lofted, × 1.6 specials, capped at 0.85 clean = (1 − missP) × (1 − edgeP)

The calibrated coefficients are missCoef 0.736 (default 0.8), edgeFit 0.429 (0.4) and edgeTiming 0.268 (0.25).

Power, speed and direction

A clean hit leaves the bat with a quality q = tq × (0.3 + 0.7·fit). Speed, launch angle and scatter all depend on q.

ground speed = power × (9 + 15·q) + paceUse × ballSpeed × q m/s lofted speed = power × (7 + 11.5·q) × 0.82 if q ≤ 0.55 direction = aim + err·5.5·drift·(ballSpeed/28) + scatter scatter = triangular(±1) × 0.202 rad × (0.8 + 0.9·(1−q)) × (ballSpeed/28) cal 0.16→0.202

After the bat

The ball flies under gravity with light air drag. Each bounce on the outfield keeps 36% of its vertical speed. A skidding ball keeps its pace and a steep one loses up to 45%. Rolling friction is 2.6 + 0.14·v m/s². Crossing the rope in the air is a 6; along the ground it's a 4. The rope is about 30 m straight, 20 m square and 14 m fine.

Edges

When the edge roll hits, the kind of edge is picked by rules (not randomly), then launched within that kind's range. Edged balls have q = 0.1.

Which edge

  1. Specials: 60% top edge, otherwise outside if late, inside if early.
  2. Short ball (short or bouncer) with a pull, a front-foot shot or a lofted shot: top edge.
  3. Early (>1σ) straight, cover or on drive to a ball seaming in (seam < −4 cm): leading edge.
  4. Late (err > 10 ms) or a ball moving away and arriving outside off: outside edge.
  5. Otherwise an inside edge. If the ball was hitting the stumps, there's a 50% chance it's played on (bowled).

Where edges go (calibrated)

Directions are degrees from straight toward the off side, so 180° is straight at the keeper. The outside edge's speed is a share of the ball's pace. A top edge goes to either side of the wicket.

Fielding, catches and runs

Each fielder (and the keeper) scans the ball's path for the earliest point they can reach at each level of effort, starting from where they actually are at contact. Every fielder takes their easiest option that isn't much later than their earliest. The fielder who gets there first, after a small cost for harder efforts, plays the ball.

Catch chance = skill × effort. Skill is Easy 0.85 / Medium 0.92 / Hard 0.96, ×0.93 for the slips and +0.05 for the keeper. ×0.85 more if the ball is faster than 20 m/s, and ×0.85 more if it's above 2 m. A catchable ball is in the air between 0.12 m and 2.45 m.

Runs

fielder time = 0.32 s reaction + distance / speed (+0.3 s to accelerate) throw arrives = gather + 0.38 s + distance to keeper / 21 m/s + 0.12 s runs = floor((throw arrives − 0.2 − 0.25) / 1.95 s), max 3

Fielder speed is Easy 5.0 / Medium 5.6 / Hard 6.1 m/s, and the keeper runs at 75% of that. A defensive shot with q < 0.5 never runs. A drop goes to ground at the fielder's feet with 0.9 s to gather. There are no run-outs or byes: the batters only run what's safe.

What actually happens: simulated

The wrong shot, perfectly timed

What stands out in this calibration:
  • Perfectly timed drives to their ideal ball are almost always 4s (straight 98%, cover 92%). Lofted, they're 6s about 86% of the time.
  • A perfect square cut to a chest-high ball is caught about 14% of the time. The flat slap starts above 1 m, so it's catchable in the air before it bounces, and point is right there.
  • A perfect forward defence still gets a single every time: only a defence with q < 0.5 is blocked from running.
  • One sigma late is costly with the specials: sweep 47% out, scoop 51% out.
